# 🧠 Core Concept

Every Time & Work problem revolves around only **three things**.

```text
               Work
                 ▲
                 │
      Efficiency × Time
```

These three quantities are always related.

If any **two values** are known, the third can always be calculated.

---

# 📐 Formula Triangle

## Formula

```text
Work = Efficiency × Time
```

## Rearranging the Formula

```text
Efficiency = Work ÷ Time
```

```text
Time = Work ÷ Efficiency
```

---

# 📦 What is Work?

Work means **the total task to be completed**.

Examples:

- Painting a wall
- Filling a tank
- Building a house
- Writing a report
- Digging a pit

In aptitude questions, the actual size of the work is usually **not important**.

Instead, we assume a convenient value (usually the **LCM**) to simplify calculations.

---

# ⏰ What is Time?

Time is the duration required to complete a given work.

Example

If A completes a work in **10 days**,

then

```text
Time = 10 Days
```

---

# ⚡ What is Efficiency?

Efficiency means

> **The amount of work completed in one day.**

Think of efficiency as a person's **speed of working**.

---

## Real-Life Example

Suppose two painters are hired.

| Worker | Time Required |
|---------|---------------|
| A | 10 Days |
| B | 20 Days |

Who is faster?

Obviously,

✅ Worker **A**

But aptitude never asks

> Who is faster?

Instead, it asks

> **How much work does A complete in one day?**

That quantity is called **Efficiency**.

---

## Efficiency Formula

```text
Efficiency = Work Done in One Day
```

If

```text
A completes one work in 10 days
```

then

```text
Efficiency = 1/10
```

Meaning

```text
Every day,

A completes

1/10 of the total work.
```

---

# 💡 Why Efficiency is More Important than Days?

Most beginners focus on

```text
10 Days

15 Days

20 Days
```

Instead,

always convert days into

```text
Efficiency
```

because

✔ Efficiencies can be added.

❌ Days cannot be added.

---

# ⭐ Golden Rule

More Efficiency

⬇

Less Time

---

Less Efficiency

⬇

More Time

---

## Easy Example

| Worker | Days | Efficiency |
|---------|------|------------|
| A | 5 | 1/5 |
| B | 10 | 1/10 |

Since

```text
1/5 > 1/10
```

A is **more efficient**.

Therefore,

```text
Less Time ⇒ More Efficiency

More Time ⇒ Less Efficiency
```

---

# 🔥 Why Do We Use LCM?

This is the most important concept in Time & Work.

Suppose

```text
A = 15 Days

B = 10 Days
```

Actual efficiencies are

```text
A = 1/15

B = 1/10
```

Adding fractions repeatedly becomes lengthy and increases the chance of mistakes.

So,

we choose a convenient Total Work.

---

## Step 1

Find the LCM.

```text
LCM(15,10)

=

30
```

---

## Step 2

Assume

```text
Total Work = 30 Units
```

---

## Step 3

Find Efficiency

```text
A

=

30 ÷ 15

=

2 Units/Day
```

```text
B

=

30 ÷ 10

=

3 Units/Day
```

Now calculations become much easier.

---

# ❓Why Doesn't Assuming LCM Change the Answer?

Imagine

```text
1 Meter

=

100 Centimeters
```

The length remains the same.

Only the **unit** changes.

Similarly,

```text
1 Complete Work

=

30 Units
```

The amount of work remains exactly the same.

Only the unit of measurement changes.

Therefore,

assuming Total Work = LCM **never changes the final answer**.

It only makes calculations easier.

# 📝 Universal Problem Solving Framework

Every Time & Work question follows the same thought process.

```text
Read the Question
        │
        ▼
Write the Given Data
        │
        ▼
Find the LCM
        │
        ▼
Assume Total Work = LCM
        │
        ▼
Find Individual Efficiencies
        │
        ▼
Combine Efficiencies
        │
        ▼
Find Work Done
        │
        ▼
Find Remaining Work (If Required)
        │
        ▼
Update Efficiency (If Someone Leaves/Joins)
        │
        ▼
Calculate the Final Answer
```

---

# 🎯 Golden Rules

### ✅ Rule 1

Always convert **Days → Efficiency**.

---

### ✅ Rule 2

Add **Efficiencies**, not **Days**.

---

### ✅ Rule 3

Whenever possible,

take

```text
Total Work = LCM
```

---

### ✅ Rule 4

If someone leaves,

remove that person's efficiency.

---

### ✅ Rule 5

If someone joins,

add that person's efficiency.

---

### ✅ Rule 6

Leak or destructive work is always treated as **negative efficiency**.


```text

```

---

### ❌ Mistake 2

---

---


- Understanding the concept is more important than memorizing formulas.

# 👥 Two Person Work

> **Difficulty:** ⭐☆☆☆☆
>
> **Asked In:** TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Capgemini, Accenture

---

# 📖 Introduction

The most common Time & Work questions involve **two people working together**.

The goal is to determine:

- How much work each person completes in one day (Efficiency).
- Their combined efficiency.
- The total time required to complete the work together.

Once we understand this concept, it becomes the foundation for solving advanced topics such as:

- Remaining Work
- Leaving & Joining
- Alternate Day Work
- Pipes & Cisterns

---

# 🧠 Core Idea

Imagine two workers building a wall.

| Worker | Time Required |
|---------|---------------|
| A | 15 Days |
| B | 10 Days |

Instead of thinking,

> A needs 15 days.

we think,

> **How much work does A complete every day?**

This is called **Efficiency**.

---

# 📦 Problem Pattern

```text
A completes a work in X days.

B completes the same work in Y days.

Find the time taken together.
```

---

# 🚀 Universal Approach

Every Two Person Work problem follows exactly the same steps.

```text
Read Question
      │
      ▼
Write Given Data
      │
      ▼
Find LCM
      │
      ▼
Assume Total Work = LCM
      │
      ▼
Find Individual Efficiency
      │
      ▼
Add Efficiencies
      │
      ▼
Find Time
```

---

# 📝 Solved Example

## Question

A can complete a work in **15 days**.

B can complete the same work in **10 days**.

If both work together,

**in how many days will the work be completed?**

---

# ✅ Step 1 — Write the Given Data

```text
A → 15 Days

B → 10 Days
```

### 🤔 Why?

Before solving,

organize the information clearly.

This helps avoid mistakes later.

---

# ✅ Step 2 — Find the LCM

```text
LCM(15,10)

=

30
```

### 🤔 Why?

Actual efficiencies are

```text
1/15

1/10
```

Fractions make calculations lengthy.

Instead,

assume

```text
Total Work = 30 Units
```

Now everything becomes simple.

---

# ✅ Step 3 — Find Individual Efficiency

Formula

```text
Efficiency

=

Total Work

÷

Days
```

For A

```text
30 ÷15

=

2 Units/Day
```

For B

```text
30 ÷10

=

3 Units/Day
```

### 🤔 Why?

Efficiency tells us

> **How much work a person completes in one day.**

Without efficiency,

we cannot combine workers.

---

# ✅ Step 4 — Find Combined Efficiency

```text
A

↓

2 Units

+

B

↓

3 Units

=

5 Units/Day
```

### 🤔 Why?

Both workers are working **simultaneously**.

So,

their daily work gets added.

> 💡 Add **Efficiency**, NOT Days.

---

# ✅ Step 5 — Find Time

Formula

```text
Time

=

Total Work

÷

Combined Efficiency
```

Calculation

```text
30

÷

5

=

6 Days
```

---

# ✅ Final Answer

```text
A and B together complete the work

in

6 Days.
```

---

# 🧠 Why Does This Method Always Work?

Think of filling buckets.

Suppose

A fills

```text
2 Buckets/Day
```

and

B fills

```text
3 Buckets/Day
```

Together,

they fill

```text
5 Buckets/Day
```

Exactly the same idea is used in Time & Work.

---

# ⚡ Shortcut Formula

If

```text
A = x Days

B = y Days
```

Then

```text
Time Together

=

(x × y)

÷

(x + y)
```

---

## Example

```text
A =15

B =10
```

```text
15×10

÷

25

=

6 Days
```

---

# ❓Should We Always Use This Shortcut?

### YES ✅

When

- Only two workers are given.
- Both start together.
- No one leaves.
- No one joins.
- No alternate day condition.

---

### NO ❌

Avoid this shortcut when

- Someone leaves.
- Someone joins.
- Remaining Work is involved.
- Three workers are present.
- Alternate Day questions.
- Pipes & Cisterns.

In these cases,

always use the **LCM Method**.

---

# 🎯 Interview Tip

Most placement exams accept **both methods**.

However,

learning the **LCM Method** first is better because

it works for almost **every type** of Time & Work question.

---

# 📖 Introduction

In the previous chapter, we learned how to solve questions involving **two workers**.

In this chapter, we extend the same concept to **three workers**.

There are two common patterns asked in placement exams:

- **Type 1:** All three workers' individual times are given.
- **Type 2:** Pair efficiencies `(A+B)`, `(B+C)`, `(A+C)` are given.

The second pattern is one of the most frequently asked in aptitude exams.

---

# 🎯 Type 1 — Three Workers Working Together

## Problem Pattern

```text
A completes a work in X days.

B completes the work in Y days.

C completes the work in Z days.

Find the time taken together.
```

---

## Approach

```text
Find LCM
      │
      ▼
Assume Total Work = LCM
      │
      ▼
Find Individual Efficiencies
      │
      ▼
Add All Efficiencies
      │
      ▼
Find Time
```

---

# 📝 Solved Example

## Question

A completes a work in **12 days**.

B completes it in **18 days**.

C completes it in **36 days**.

Find the time taken by all three together.

---

## Step 1 — Write the Given Data

```text
A → 12 Days

B → 18 Days

C → 36 Days
```

### 🤔 Why?

Organizing the given data makes the solution easier and avoids confusion.

---

## Step 2 — Find the LCM

```text
LCM(12,18,36)

=

36
```

Assume

```text
Total Work = 36 Units
```

### 🤔 Why?

LCM converts fractional efficiencies into whole numbers.

---

## Step 3 — Find Individual Efficiency

```text
A

=

36 ÷12

=

3 Units/Day
```

```text
B

=

36 ÷18

=

2 Units/Day
```

```text
C

=

36 ÷36

=

1 Unit/Day
```

---

## Step 4 — Add All Efficiencies

```text
3 +2 +1

=

6 Units/Day
```

### 🤔 Why?

All three workers are working simultaneously.

Hence,

their daily work gets added.

---

## Step 5 — Find Time

```text
Time

=

36

÷

6

=

6 Days
```

---

# ✅ Final Answer

```text
All three workers together complete the work

in

6 Days.
```

# 🔥 Type 2 — Pair Efficiency Questions

This is one of the favorite patterns in placement exams.

Instead of giving individual times,

the question gives

```text
A+B

B+C

A+C
```

---

## Problem Pattern

```text
A+B = 15 Days

B+C = 10 Days

A+C = 12 Days

Find the time taken by A+B+C together.
```

---

# 🤔 Why Are Pair Questions Different?

We don't know

- A individually
- B individually
- C individually

We only know

their combined efficiencies.

So,

we need another approach.

---

# 💡 The Most Important Concept

Suppose

```text
A+B

+

B+C

+

A+C
```

Let's expand it.

```text
(A+B)

+

(B+C)

+

(A+C)
```

Grouping similar terms,

```text
A+A

+

B+B

+

C+C
```

which becomes

```text
2A

+

2B

+

2C
```

Taking 2 common,

```text
2(A+B+C)
```

---

# ⭐ Golden Formula

```text
(A+B)

+

(B+C)

+

(A+C)

=

2(A+B+C)
```

This formula is the heart of pair-efficiency questions.

---

# 📝 Solved Example

## Question

```text
A+B = 15 Days

B+C = 10 Days

A+C = 12 Days
```

Find the time taken by all three together.

---

## Step 1 — Convert Days into Efficiency

Find the LCM.

```text
LCM(15,10,12)

=

60
```

Assume

```text
Total Work = 60 Units
```

---

## Step 2 — Find Pair Efficiencies

```text
A+B

=

60 ÷15

=

4
```

```text
B+C

=

60 ÷10

=

6
```

```text
A+C

=

60 ÷12

=

5
```

---

## Step 3 — Add Pair Efficiencies

```text
4 +6 +5

=

15
```

---

## Step 4 — Apply the Formula

```text
2(A+B+C)

=

15
```

Therefore,

```text
A+B+C

=

15

÷2

=

7.5 Units/Day
```

### 🤔 Why Divide by 2?

Because every worker appears **twice**.

Look carefully.

```text
A+B

A+C

↓

A appears twice.
```

```text
A+B

B+C

↓

B appears twice.
```

```text
A+C

B+C

↓

C appears twice.
```

Hence,

the total efficiency is counted **two times**.

So,

divide by **2**.

---

## Step 5 — Find Time

```text
Time

=

60

÷

7.5

=

8 Days
```

---

# ✅ Final Answer

```text
A+B+C together complete the work

in

8 Days.
```


```text

(A+B+C)

```


```text

=

7.5

−6

```

```text

=

−5

2.5 Units/Day
```

```text
C

=

7.5

−4

=

3.5 Units/Day
```

---

# 🧠 Why Does This Formula Work?

Suppose

```text
ABC

=

A+B+C
```

If we subtract

```text
B+C
```

then only

```text
A
```

remains.

The same logic applies to B and C.

---

# ⚡ Interview Shortcut

Whenever you see

```text
A+B

B+C

A+C
```

Immediately think

```text
(A+B)+(B+C)+(A+C)

=

2(A+B+C)
```

This saves a lot of time in placement exams.

# 🔄 Remaining Work, Leaving & Joining

> **Difficulty:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆
>
> **Asked In:** Infosys, TCS, Accenture, Wipro, Cognizant, Deloitte

---

# 📖 Introduction

In the previous chapters, we solved problems where **all workers continued until the work was completed**.

But in real placement questions,

- Someone may leave.
- Someone may join.
- Work may stop halfway.
- Only part of the work may be completed.

These questions are solved using the **same LCM method**, but we divide the work into **multiple phases**.

---

# 🎯 Core Idea

Whenever the workers change,

**split the work into different phases.**

```text
Phase 1
(A + B)
     │
     ▼
Work Completed
     │
     ▼
Remaining Work
     │
     ▼
Phase 2
(Only B)
     │
     ▼
Finish Work
```

> 💡 **Golden Rule**
>
> Whenever a worker joins or leaves,
> **the efficiency changes**.
>
> Always calculate the **new efficiency** before solving the next phase.

---

# 📦 Important Formulas

## Work Done

```text
Work Done = Efficiency × Time
```

---

## Remaining Work

```text
Remaining Work

=

Total Work

−

Completed Work
```

---

## Remaining Time

```text
Remaining Time

=

Remaining Work

÷

Current Efficiency
```

---

```text
36
```

Assume

```text

```

---

```text
A

=

36 ÷12

=

3 Units/Day
```

```text
B

=

36 ÷18

2 Units/Day
```

---

# ✅ Step 4 — Combined Efficiency

```text
3 +2

=

5 Units/Day
```

Total Work

# 🧠 Why Did We Change the Efficiency?

Initially,

both workers were working.

After A leaves,

only B continues.

Therefore,

the daily work changes.

Always use the **current workers' efficiency**.

# 🔄 Alternate Day Problems

## Problem Pattern

```
A works on Day 1

B works on Day 2

Again A

Again B

...

Find total time.
```

---

## Core Idea

Workers are **not working together**.

They work **one after another**.

So,

first find

```
Work completed in one complete cycle.
```

Then

calculate

```
Remaining Work.
```

Finally,

find the last required day.

---

## Example

A completes a work in **12 days**

B completes it in **18 days**

They work on alternate days.

A starts first.

Find total time.

---

### Solution

LCM

```
36
```

Efficiency

```
A =3

B =2
```

One cycle

```
Day 1 →3

Day 2 →2

Total

=5 Units
```

Remaining work is calculated cycle by cycle until less than one day's work remains.

---

## Shortcut

Always calculate

```
One Complete Cycle

↓

Remaining Work

↓

Last Day
```

Never calculate one day at a time.

# 🎯 Final Placement Strategy

Whenever you read a Time & Work question, ask yourself these **6 questions**:

1. What is the Total Work? (Usually LCM)
2. What is each worker's Efficiency?
3. Who is working in this phase?
4. Is anyone joining or leaving?
5. Is there any remaining work?
6. Which method applies? (LCM, Pair, Phase, Cycle, Ratio, or Net Efficiency)

If you answer these six questions in order, you can solve almost every Time & Work problem asked in placement exams.
